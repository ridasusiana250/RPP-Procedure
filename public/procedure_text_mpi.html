🌟 Communicative Purpose in the Workshop

Why do motorcycle mechanics read Procedure Texts? Simple: to guide you step-by-step so repairs are done right, engines run smoothly, and riders stay safe on the road.

Real-Life Workshop Reasons:
  • Guiding mechanics to service motorcycle engines according to official manufacturer standards (Honda, Yamaha, Suzuki, Kawasaki).
  • Preventing expensive blunders like stripped crankcase threads, bent valves, or hydraulic brake failure.
  • Protecting yourself and workshop friends from hot oil burns, toxic solvents, and battery acid (K3).
  • Ensuring every customer's motorcycle is 100% safe, reliable, and fuel-efficient.

💡 Mechanic Golden Rule: In our workshop, following the service manual protects lives and saves engines!

🎯 Clear, Direct Tone for Mechanics

Workshop manuals speak plainly so mechanics can work fast without confusion:

Direct & Short (No Fluff): Straight to the point. We tell the mechanic what to do, what tool to grab, and the exact torque number.
Strict Step Order (Chronological): Step 1 must come before Step 2. You cannot bleed a brake before pouring new brake fluid!
Action Commands: Starts with basic action verbs: Inspect, Tighten, Measure, Clean, Loosen, Bleed.

How to Inspect, Clean, and Gap a Motorcycle Spark Plug
Tools, Equipment & Materials:
  • 16 mm spark plug socket wrench & ratchet handle
  • Precision feeler gauge (blade thickness 0.80 mm – 0.90 mm)
  • Brass wire brush and brake/carburetor parts cleaner
  • Calibrated torque wrench set to 16 N·m
Step-by-Step Workshop Instructions:
  1. First, disconnect the spark plug high-tension cap and blow away dirt from the cylinder head cavity using compressed air.
  2. Next, unscrew and remove the spark plug counter-clockwise using a 16 mm spark plug socket wrench.
  3. Then, inspect the electrode color for carbon buildup, and clean stubborn soot deposits gently with a brass wire brush.
  4. After that, measure the spark plug gap with a feeler gauge, and adjust the side electrode carefully to 0.85 mm.
  5. Finally, thread the plug by hand clockwise into the cylinder head, and tighten it to exactly 16 N·m with a torque wrench.

🔤 1. Imperative Sentences & Workshop Action Verbs

In a busy workshop, instructions are commands. We skip the word "You" and jump straight to the base action verb (Bare Infinitive):

❌ You should consider tightening the drain bolt now. (Too slow and wordy!)

✅ Tighten the drain bolt to 24 N·m using a torque wrench. (Direct & Clear!)

❌ Don't open the hot radiator cap with bare hands.

✅ Do not open the radiator cap while the engine is hot. (Direct Safety Warning!)

⏳ 2. Connectives & Temporal Words (Urutan Waktu)

These signal words keep your disassembly and assembly stages neat and organized:

Start: First, Initially, Before starting
Middle: Next, Then, After that, Subsequently
Simultaneous: While, Meanwhile, At the same time
Final Step: Finally, Lastly, Before test riding

📍 3. Adverbial Phrases (Cara, Waktu, & Ukuran)

Tells the mechanic HOW, WHERE, or TO WHAT MEASUREMENT to perform the action:

  • Manner (Cara): Rotate the adjustment screw clockwise slowly.
  • Measurement & Time (Ukuran & Waktu): Idle the engine for 3 minutes at 1,400 RPM.
  • Place & Tool (Tempat & Kunci): Insert the feeler gauge between the spark plug electrodes.

⚡ 4. Action Verbs for Motorcycle Mechanics

Top technical action verbs found in official Honda, Yamaha, and Suzuki service manuals:

Key Mechanic Verbs: Torque, loosen, tighten, measure, inspect, clean, flush, bleed, calibrate, lubricate, align, disconnect.
